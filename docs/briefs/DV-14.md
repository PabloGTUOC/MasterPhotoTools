## Brief

### Why

After DV-12 and DV-13, Develop's neutral matches Apple's rendering (what Photomator uses) in tone, colour,
frame and geometry. The remaining difference is **detail**. The architect developed nine RAWs at Develop's
defaults (noise reduction 25, capture sharpening 50): seven of the owner's XZ-1 ORFs, a Nikon D610 NEF and
the X-Pro2 chart RAF. Each was compared with Apple's rendering by contrast in four frequency bands, split
into detailed and flat areas. **Gain** is Apple's contrast over ours, where 1.0 means equal.

| | Detail, < 0.5 px | 0.5–1 px | 1–2 px | 2–4 px | Flat-area noise |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| XZ-1, ISO 400 (6 files) | 0.91–1.09 | 1.01–1.20 | **1.13–1.20** | 1.07–1.14 | equal (0.92–1.07) |
| XZ-1, ISO 200 | 1.15 | 1.18 | 1.19 | 1.11 | ours **1.4×** too smooth |
| D610, ISO 100 | **1.33** | 1.27 | 1.19 | 1.08 | ours **1.7×** too smooth |

Two causes, each confirmed by experiment:

1. **Apple adds a mild sharpening at 1–4 px**, on top of the finest scale, which our capture sharpening
   already matches. A plain unsharp mask can't reproduce it, because it also sharpens the finest scale. A
   band-limited one can: luminance + 0.3 × (blur σ 1 − blur σ 3). With it, the mean detail mismatch
   on the XZ-1 files falls from 0.108 to 0.045, and flat areas barely change.
2. **Noise reduction 25 is a fixed strength; Apple's follows the noise in the photograph.** Our filter's
   threshold is absolute, so the same slider value smooths a clean ISO 100 file, or a camera whose raw
   data sits darker, far harder than a noisy one. The best match to Apple, file by file: XZ-1 ISO 400 → 25,
   XZ-1 ISO 200 → about 10, D610 ISO 100 → about 8.
   - Measuring each photograph's noise and scaling the strength by it reproduces this. The XZ-1 at ISO 400
     measures 0.0045–0.0055, so the reference is **0.005**. The ISO 200 file measures 0.0035 and the D610
     0.0015.
   - The rule effective = 25 × noise / 0.005 then puts **every** XZ-1 file and the D610 within
     0.029–0.064 of Apple's detail and 0.79–1.15 of its noise. At the fixed 25 the D610 was 0.109 and
     1.66.
   - It is exact in principle. On synthetic noise from 0.0025 to 0.02, the scaled noise reduction keeps
     the same share of the noise (0.209) at every level; the fixed one keeps 0.149 to 0.724.

The X-Pro2 (X-Trans) is still too smooth even with noise reduction off: 1.28× short at the finest scale.
That is its demosaic, and it is a separate step.

### What to build

All in `crates/rollchrome-core` (G1).

**1. Measure the noise.** New module `media/develop/noise.rs`:
- `pub const NOISE_REFERENCE: f32 = 0.005;`. Its doc comment gives the reason: the median of the owner's
  XZ-1 files at ISO 400, measured as below after the camera's geometry, the files on which DV-12 tuned 25.
- `pub fn noise_level(pixels: &[[f32; 3]], width: u32, height: u32) -> f32`. It works on
  `y = sqrt(max(0, 0.288040·R + 0.711874·G + 0.000086·B))`, ProPhoto's luminance weights, for every
  pixel. For every 2×2 block whose top-left is at even (x, y), with x + 1 < width and y + 1 < height:
  `d = |y(x,y) − y(x+1,y) − y(x,y+1) + y(x+1,y+1)| / 2`. The result is **median(d) / 0.6745**: Donoho's
  robust estimate, which ignores edges because they are a minority of blocks.
  - Use `select_nth_unstable_by` for the median, not a full sort. Parallelise the collection over rows
    with rayon if you like; the result must not depend on it.
  - If width < 2, height < 2 or `pixels.len() != width × height`, return `NOISE_REFERENCE`, meaning
    "no scaling".
- `pub fn effective_noise_reduction(amount: f32, level: f32) -> f32`. It returns
  `(amount × level / NOISE_REFERENCE).clamp(0.0, 100.0)`. If `level` is not finite or is < 0, it returns
  `amount` unchanged. `amount == 0.0` gives 0.0.

**2. Use it wherever Develop reduces noise**, so the output and the preview agree:
- In `develop_one` (`tools/develop.rs`): after `apply_camera_geometry` and before `reduce_noise`, compute
  `noise_level` on `linear` and pass `effective_noise_reduction(action.noise_reduction, level)` to
  `reduce_noise` instead of `action.noise_reduction`. That covers every look; film simulations included.
- In `DevelopPreview` (`media/develop/preview.rs`): add a field `noise_level: f32`. Set it in `from_linear`
  from the full-resolution `linear` it receives, **before** downscaling, since downscaling removes noise.
  `new_with_lens` already applies the camera geometry before calling `from_linear`. In
  `render_with_data_dir`, keep the 0–100 validation on the requested `noise_reduction` as it is, then pass
  `effective_noise_reduction(noise_reduction, self.noise_level)` to `reduce_noise`.
- `reduce_noise` itself does not change.

**3. Output sharpening for the neutral picture.** In `media/develop/sharpen.rs`:
- `pub const OUTPUT_SHARPEN: f32 = 0.3;` with a doc comment giving the reason (the fit above).
- `pub fn output_sharpen(image: &mut image::ImageBuffer<image::Rgb<u16>, Vec<u16>>)`. It works on the
  developed 16-bit ProPhoto image, ROMM-encoded, after the tone curve:
  1. `Y = (0.288040·R + 0.711874·G + 0.000086·B) / 65535` per pixel.
  2. `b1` = Gaussian blur of Y with σ **1.0**, and `b3` with σ **3.0**, in full-resolution pixels. Use
     separable kernels of radius ⌈3σ⌉, normalised, clamped at the edges, as the existing `blur` in the
     same file does. Reuse it.
  3. `d = OUTPUT_SHARPEN × (b1 − b3) × 65535`. Add the **same** `d` to R, G and B, round, and clamp to
     0..=65535. The same delta on all three keeps a grey pixel grey.
  - It runs at full resolution, before any `long_edge` downscale.
- Call it in two places, on the neutral picture only: in `develop_one`'s `ResolvedLook::Neutral` branch
  (with `ToneCurve::Standard`), on `developed.image` before rendering, and at the end of `develop_neutral`
  in `media/develop/mod.rs`, so `develop_neutral` stays the definition of the neutral picture. LUT looks
  and film simulations do **not** get it: they have their own character, and a film's grain must never be
  sharpened.
- The preview does not apply it, as it does not apply capture sharpening (DV-10). At preview size its
  effect is below a pixel.

### Tests

Expected names; the count is what matters (see the table). For synthetic noise, use a small deterministic
generator in the test (an LCG with Box–Muller, as `develop_tests.rs` has).

`noise.rs` unit tests:
1. `noise_level_recovers_the_noise_put_in`: a 400×300 image where each pixel is `v = (0.5 + n)²` in all
   three channels, with n Gaussian of σ s. The weights sum to 1, so sqrt-luminance is 0.5 + n. For s = 0.0025
   and s = 0.01, `noise_level` is within **3 %** of s.
2. `a_flat_or_tiny_image_has_no_noise_or_no_scaling`: a constant image gives 0.0; a 1×N or N×1 image, or one
   whose pixel count is wrong, gives `NOISE_REFERENCE`.
3. `the_effective_amount_follows_the_noise`: (25, 0.005) → 25; (25, 0.0025) → 12.5; (25, 0.01) → 50;
   (25, 0.05) → 100, clamped; (0, 0.01) → 0; (25, f32::NAN) → 25; (25, −1) → 25. Within 1e-4.
4. `noise_reduction_scaled_to_the_noise_keeps_the_same_share_of_it`: images as in test 1 with s = 0.0025,
   0.005 and 0.01. Kept share = std of sqrt-luminance after `reduce_noise(.., effective_noise_reduction(25,
   noise_level(..)), 1.0)` over std before. The architect measured 0.208, 0.209 and 0.209. Assert all three
   are within **0.02** of each other and between 0.15 and 0.27. Then assert that with a fixed 25 the shares
   at s = 0.0025 and s = 0.01 differ by more than **0.2**; the architect measured 0.149 and 0.422.

`sharpen.rs` unit tests:
5. `output_sharpening_leaves_a_flat_image_unchanged`: a 64×16 image of one grey value is unchanged, exactly.
6. `output_sharpening_lifts_both_sides_of_an_edge_and_keeps_grey`: 64×16, every row
   `[19661; 32]` then `[39321; 32]`, the same in R, G and B. After `output_sharpen`, row 8 must read, within
   ±2: x = 0 → 19661, 28 → 18953, 30 → 18192, 31 → 18877, 32 → 40105, 33 → 40790, 35 → 40029,
   63 → 39321. R = G = B at every pixel.

**Existing tests:** tests that develop a synthetic DNG with noise reduction (`develop_tests.rs`,
`film_tests.rs`, `develop_output_tests.rs`, `develop_desktop.rs`) now get an effective amount scaled by
that DNG's measured noise. They should still hold, since they assert "differs" or orderings. Tests that
compare Develop's neutral output with `develop_neutral` hold because both now sharpen. If one fails,
**report it with its output and do not edit it**; the architect decides.

### Not in this step

- **X-Trans detail.** Its demosaic is softer than Apple's even with noise reduction off. That's a
  separate step, which wants the owner's X100VI files.
- Any change to the screen: the slider keeps 0–100 and its default of 25. Output sharpening for looks or
  films. The Edit screen's own noise reduction, which is a different tool.
- The owner's photographs are not in the repository. The architect measures the result on them in review:
  on the seven XZ-1 files and the D610 at the defaults, mean detail mismatch within **0.07** of Apple's
  and flat-area noise within **0.75–1.25** of it. The prototype measured 0.029–0.064 and 0.79–1.15.

### How to work: every step costs money

The build is paid for by the step. These rules are part of the brief:

1. **Run every command in the foreground and wait for it to return.** Never start a command in the
   background and check on it, and never loop or sleep waiting for one. A long build is one step if you
   wait for it.
2. **While working, run only what covers the change:** `cargo check -p rollchrome-core`,
   `cargo test -p rollchrome-core --lib noise`, `--lib sharpen`, and
   `cargo test -p rollchrome-core --test develop_tests --test develop_output_tests`.
3. **Run the full list under Gates once, at the end.** If one gate fails, fix it and re-run that gate,
   not the list.
4. **Read each file once**, using a line range or `grep` for large ones such as `tools/develop.rs` and
   `preview.rs`. Do not re-read a file to confirm an edit you just made.
5. **Do not change approach silently.** If the same failure survives two attempts, stop, say what you
   tried in the Report, and commit what works.
